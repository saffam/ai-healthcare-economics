# Screening Attendance and the Economic Value of AI-Assisted Diabetic Retinopathy Screening

## 1. Purpose

This analysis examines how screening attendance and follow-up affect the potential clinical and economic value of AI-assisted diabetic retinopathy screening.

## 2. Importance of attendance

The effectiveness of a screening programme depends not only on the accuracy of its diagnostic technology but also on whether eligible people attend screening.

An AI system cannot identify disease in people who do not participate in the screening process.

Consequently, improvements in diagnostic performance do not necessarily produce equivalent improvements in population health outcomes.

## 3. Screening pathway

The relevant pathway includes:

1. Identification of eligible individuals.
2. Invitation to screening.
3. Attendance.
4. Retinal image acquisition.
5. Image assessment.
6. Referral where indicated.
7. Further clinical assessment.
8. Treatment and follow-up where required.

Each stage may affect the final outcome.

## 4. Economic implications

Low attendance may reduce the number of people receiving screening and may limit the population-level benefits of an AI intervention.

High attendance may increase screening activity and therefore increase total service expenditure, even where the cost per screening assessment falls.

The economic consequences depend on screening capacity, staffing, referral activity, treatment and follow-up.

## 5. Attendance and AI implementation

AI may potentially change service capacity, assessment time or the organisation of screening work.

However, AI should not be assumed to increase attendance unless evidence demonstrates a causal relationship.

Any observed change in attendance must be distinguished from changes associated with invitations, access, service organisation, patient characteristics or wider healthcare conditions.

## 6. Follow-up and clinical outcomes

Screening is not sufficient by itself to improve health outcomes.

Abnormal findings must lead to appropriate assessment and, where necessary, treatment.

Failure to attend follow-up can reduce the potential benefits of early detection.

Economic evaluations should therefore consider the entire screening and follow-up pathway rather than diagnostic performance alone.

## 7. Implications for the economic model

The model should distinguish:

- Eligible population.
- Invited population.
- Attending population.
- People successfully screened.
- Ungradable images.
- People referred.
- People completing follow-up.

These quantities must not be treated as interchangeable.

Published NHS coverage figures may provide contextual information, but they must not be interpreted as evidence that AI has improved attendance.

## 8. Limitations

The available national screening statistics are not necessarily sufficient to establish the causal effect of AI on attendance.

The project must not claim that AI increases participation without suitable comparative evidence.

## 9. Conclusion

Screening attendance and follow-up are important determinants of the clinical and economic value of diabetic retinopathy screening.

The potential value of AI depends on how it interacts with the complete healthcare pathway, including participation, assessment, referral and treatment.

AI diagnostic accuracy alone cannot establish population-level economic value.
