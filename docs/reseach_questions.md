
# Research Questions

## Project Title

The Economics of Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare: Economic Value, Human Trust, Institutional Differences and the Governance of AI-Assisted Decision-Making

## Central Research Question

Under what economic, institutional and policy conditions can artificial intelligence improve healthcare efficiency and outcomes, how should its costs, benefits and risks be evaluated, and to what extent can healthcare professionals, patients and organisations reasonably trust AI-assisted decisions?

### Rationale

The increasing integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into healthcare presents opportunities to improve productivity, support clinical decision-making and enhance the allocation of limited healthcare resources. However, the economic and organisational value of these technologies cannot be assumed from their technical capabilities alone. Their contribution depends on implementation costs, institutional capacity, professional judgement, patient characteristics and the regulatory environment.

This research therefore examines AI as both a technological innovation and an economic and institutional intervention. It considers not only its potential benefits, but also the conditions under which those benefits can be achieved, sustained and distributed equitably.

---

## Research Question 1: Economic Value and Healthcare Efficiency

**RQ1:** Does the adoption of artificial intelligence generate measurable economic value and improvements in healthcare productivity, efficiency and patient outcomes?

### Explanation

Healthcare systems operate under resource constraints and must continually evaluate how available financial, human and technological resources can be allocated effectively. AI may contribute to efficiency through improved administrative processes, clinical decision support, resource planning and diagnostic assistance. Nevertheless, reductions in task completion time do not necessarily translate into improvements in overall productivity or patient outcomes.

This question examines the empirical evidence on the economic value of healthcare AI, distinguishing between operational efficiency, productivity improvements, clinical effectiveness and broader health system benefits.

---

## Research Question 2: Costs and Economic Evaluation

**RQ2:** What direct, indirect, implementation, maintenance and opportunity costs are associated with the adoption of AI in healthcare, and how should these be incorporated into economic evaluations?

### Explanation

Evaluating healthcare AI requires consideration of its full economic costs throughout the implementation and operational lifecycle. Beyond initial acquisition or development expenditure, organisations may incur costs associated with infrastructure, data preparation, system integration, workforce training, maintenance, monitoring and regulatory compliance.

Furthermore, the resources allocated to AI adoption have alternative uses within healthcare systems. This question investigates whether the economic benefits of AI justify its total costs and examines the importance of opportunity costs, uncertainty and long-term sustainability in economic evaluation.

---

## Research Question 3: Workforce, Professional Autonomy and Human Decision-Making

**RQ3:** How does the integration of AI into healthcare affect professional autonomy, workforce productivity, clinical responsibilities and human decision-making?

### Explanation

AI-assisted systems may transform the distribution of tasks and responsibilities within healthcare organisations. While some applications may complement professional expertise and reduce routine workloads, others may alter established decision-making processes or introduce new requirements for verification and oversight.

Particular attention is given to the interaction between human judgement and automated recommendations, including the potential for automation bias and inappropriate reliance on AI outputs. This question explores whether AI primarily complements professional capabilities, substitutes for particular tasks or changes the nature of healthcare work.

---

## Research Question 4: Trust, Reliability and Responsible Reliance

**RQ4:** What technical, human and organisational factors determine the appropriate level of trust in AI-assisted healthcare decisions?

### Explanation

Trust is an important determinant of the adoption and effective use of AI in healthcare. However, trust should be supported by evidence of reliability, safety, validity and suitability for the intended clinical context rather than by technological sophistication alone.

Healthcare professionals and patients must be able to understand the limitations of AI-assisted recommendations and retain appropriate opportunities for human review. This question examines how transparency, external validation, explainability, professional training and accountability influence trust and the responsible use of AI.

---

## Research Question 5: Institutional Differences and Transferability

**RQ5:** Why might the economic and clinical effects of the same AI technology differ across healthcare organisations, systems and population groups?

### Explanation

Healthcare organisations operate within different financial, institutional, technological and professional environments. Differences in infrastructure, workforce capabilities, organisational culture, patient populations and implementation capacity may influence the effectiveness and economic consequences of AI adoption.

Consequently, evidence of effectiveness in one setting cannot automatically be generalised to another. This question investigates the contextual factors affecting implementation and considers the extent to which findings can be transferred across hospitals, healthcare systems and population groups.

---

## Research Question 6: Policy, Governance and Accountability

**RQ6:** What policy, reimbursement, regulatory and accountability arrangements are necessary to support the safe, equitable and economically sustainable adoption of AI in healthcare?

### Explanation

The integration of AI into healthcare raises questions concerning responsibility, financial incentives, patient protection, transparency and the distribution of economic benefits and risks. Policymakers and healthcare institutions must consider how these technologies should be assessed, monitored, funded and governed throughout their operational lifecycle.

This question examines the role of regulation, health technology assessment, reimbursement mechanisms, independent evaluation and institutional accountability in ensuring that AI adoption contributes to public health objectives and the sustainable use of healthcare resources.

---

## Overall Research Contribution

Collectively, these research questions provide an interdisciplinary framework for evaluating artificial intelligence in healthcare from a health economics and policy perspective.

Rather than assuming that AI adoption necessarily produces improvements, the research investigates the economic, institutional, technological and human conditions that influence its value and consequences.

The overarching aim is to develop an evidence-based understanding of when, how and under what circumstances AI can contribute to more efficient, effective, equitable and accountable healthcare systems.

**Research approach:** Independent secondary research using published literature, official aggregate evidence and transparent economic modelling.
