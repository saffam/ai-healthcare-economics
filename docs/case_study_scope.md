# Case Study Scope: AI-Assisted Diabetic Retinopathy Screening

## 1. Purpose

This case study examines the economic, clinical and organisational implications of using artificial intelligence (AI) to support diabetic retinopathy (DR) screening.

It provides a focused application through which the wider research questions concerning economic value, productivity, human judgement, institutional differences, trust and governance can be investigated.

## 2. Why this application was selected

Diabetic retinopathy screening is an established healthcare activity involving the assessment of retinal images to identify signs of diabetic eye disease.

AI systems can analyse retinal images and classify whether further assessment or referral may be required. Depending on the system and pathway, AI may operate as an initial screening tool, a decision-support system or an automated grading system.

The application is suitable for this research because published economic evaluations have examined AI-assisted screening against human grading, including studies using cost-effectiveness and cost-utility methods.

## 3. Research focus

The case study will examine:

- The costs of AI-assisted screening compared with human grading.
- Potential changes in screening capacity and staff workload.
- Diagnostic performance, including sensitivity and specificity.
- Potential effects on referrals and follow-up.
- Long-term health outcomes, where evidence is available.
- Implementation, maintenance, training and infrastructure costs.
- The importance of patient attendance and follow-up.
- Differences between healthcare systems and population groups.
- The implications for NHS England.

## 4. Comparator

The principal comparator will be conventional human grading of retinal images.

Where a published study uses another comparator, such as no screening or a different screening pathway, that comparator will be recorded separately rather than treated as equivalent.

## 5. Geographic approach

NHS England is the principal policy and health-system reference point.

International studies will be included where they provide relevant evidence. Their findings will not automatically be transferred to NHS England.

Differences in labour costs, screening organisation, disease prevalence, referral pathways, reimbursement, technology prices and healthcare infrastructure will be considered.

## 6. Economic perspective

The primary proposed perspective is that of the healthcare system.

A societal perspective will also be considered where published evidence includes patient travel, time, lost income, informal care or other wider costs.

The research will distinguish between:

- Financial costs and savings.
- Changes in healthcare resource use.
- Released staff capacity.
- Productivity improvements.
- Health outcomes.
- Wider societal effects.

Released staff time will not automatically be treated as a cash saving.

## 7. Research design

The case study will use secondary research and transparent economic modelling.

It will not involve recruitment of patients or healthcare professionals, clinical experiments, direct healthcare interventions or access to identifiable patient records.

The model will use published evidence and appropriately sourced assumptions. Unavailable values will remain explicitly identified as missing rather than being invented.

## 8. Limitations

International findings may not be directly transferable to NHS England.

Published economic evaluations may use different populations, comparators, time horizons, model structures, cost assumptions and measures of effectiveness.

Diagnostic accuracy does not by itself establish improved patient outcomes or economic value.

The case study will therefore distinguish between observed evidence, modelled estimates and illustrative scenarios.

## 9. Relationship to the wider research

This case study supports the wider research question:

Under what economic, institutional and policy conditions can artificial intelligence improve healthcare efficiency and outcomes, how should its costs, benefits and risks be evaluated, and to what extent can healthcare professionals, patients and organisations reasonably trust AI-assisted decisions?

It is an application of the wider research framework, not a claim that findings from one clinical application apply to all healthcare AI.
