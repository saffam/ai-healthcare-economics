# Human Trust and AI-Assisted Decision-Making Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework establishes how the research will examine trust in artificial intelligence (AI) used in healthcare. It considers the relationship between AI system performance, professional judgement, patient confidence and organisational oversight.

The research does not assume that healthcare professionals or patients should automatically trust or reject AI. Instead, it examines the conditions under which trust may be justified, appropriately calibrated and maintained.

## 2. Key Concepts

### 2.1 Appropriate trust

Appropriate trust refers to the extent to which a person's reliance on an AI system is consistent with the system's demonstrated capabilities, limitations and suitability for a particular task.

Trust should be considered in relation to the specific application and decision context rather than as a general attitude towards AI.

### 2.2 Over-reliance and automation bias

Over-reliance may occur when a person gives an AI recommendation greater weight than the available evidence or their independent professional judgement warrants.

Automation bias describes the tendency to favour an automated recommendation, potentially overlooking contradictory information or errors.

The research will examine whether and under what circumstances these behaviours may affect decision quality, workflow and resource use.

### 2.3 Under-reliance

Under-reliance occurs when users do not make appropriate use of an AI system, even where its use may be justified and supported by evidence.

Potential explanations include limited confidence, poor usability, insufficient training, lack of integration into clinical workflows or previous experiences of unreliable performance.

The research will not assume that non-use represents resistance to innovation. It will consider whether non-use may be a reasonable response to uncertainty or poor system design.

## 3. Factors Influencing Trust

The analysis will consider:

- Accuracy and reliability in the intended clinical context.
- Evidence of safety and effectiveness.
- External and local validation.
- Transparency about system capabilities and limitations.
- Explainability and the communication of uncertainty.
- Usability and integration into existing workflows.
- Training and professional competence.
- Human oversight and the ability to question or override recommendations.
- Clear accountability for decisions.
- Patient communication and informed involvement.
- Monitoring, feedback and procedures for responding to errors.

## 4. Economic Implications

Trust may influence the economic value of healthcare AI through several pathways:

- Appropriate use may support more efficient decision-making.
- Over-reliance may contribute to avoidable errors, additional investigations or inappropriate resource use.
- Under-reliance may reduce the benefits expected from implementation.
- Training and oversight require financial and staff resources.
- Poorly calibrated trust may affect implementation, adoption and long-term sustainability.

These relationships will be assessed using published evidence. They will not be assumed to be causal unless the underlying study design supports a causal interpretation.

## 5. Professional and Patient Perspectives

The research will distinguish between:

- Healthcare professionals' trust in AI-supported recommendations.
- Patients' understanding and confidence in AI-assisted care.
- Organisational confidence in the safety and economic value of AI systems.
- Public confidence in the institutions responsible for adopting and overseeing AI.

These perspectives may differ and should not be treated as interchangeable.

## 6. Evidence Requirements

Relevant evidence may include:

- Studies of professional and patient attitudes towards healthcare AI.
- Research on automation bias and human factors.
- Evaluations of AI-assisted clinical workflows.
- Studies of explainability, usability and human oversight.
- Published evidence on errors, safety and decision quality.
- Guidance on accountability and responsible AI use.

For each source, the research will record the population, setting, AI application, research design and limitations.

## 7. Analytical Principles

The research will:

1. Distinguish trust from demonstrated system performance.
2. Avoid assuming that greater trust necessarily produces better outcomes.
3. Avoid assuming that lower trust necessarily represents an obstacle.
4. Consider the consequences of both over-reliance and under-reliance.
5. Examine how professional judgement and AI recommendations interact.
6. Consider whether human oversight is meaningful and practically achievable.
7. Distinguish individual behaviour from organisational and system-level factors.

## 8. Limitations

Trust is a complex concept and may be measured differently across studies. Findings from one clinical application or healthcare system may not transfer directly to another.

The project will therefore avoid treating trust as a single, universally measurable outcome.
