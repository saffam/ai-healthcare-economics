# Quantitative Analysis Plan

## 1. Purpose

This document outlines the proposed quantitative analysis methods for the independent research project.

The analysis will be based on documented secondary evidence and appropriately licensed or reusable aggregate data.

## 2. Initial Descriptive Analysis

The first stage will summarise the collected literature.

Potential summaries include:

- Number of studies recorded.
- Distribution of studies by publication year.
- Distribution of studies by research question.
- Distribution by country or healthcare setting, where recorded.
- Distribution by AI application, where recorded.
- Distribution by study design, where recorded.

These summaries will describe the evidence base and will not be interpreted as measures of AI effectiveness.

## 3. Data Validation

Before analysis, datasets will be checked for:

- Missing values.
- Duplicate records.
- Inconsistent categories.
- Incorrect data types.
- Inconsistent units.
- Unexpected values.
- Missing source documentation.

Any exclusions or transformations will be documented.

## 4. Economic Analysis

Subject to evidence availability, the project may examine:

- Total costs.
- Cost categories.
- Cost per task.
- Changes in staff time.
- Resource utilisation.
- Service capacity.
- Budget impact.
- Incremental costs between scenarios.

The choice of measures will depend on the selected healthcare application and the quality of available evidence.

## 5. Comparative Analysis

Comparisons will only be made where the underlying studies or datasets use sufficiently compatible definitions, populations, settings and measures.

Differences between studies will be documented.

Results from different healthcare systems will not automatically be treated as directly comparable.

## 6. Visualisation

Charts and tables may be used to communicate:

- Distribution of evidence.
- Changes over time.
- Differences between documented cost categories.
- Scenario-based model outputs.
- Uncertainty and sensitivity analysis.

Every visualisation will identify its data source, units and relevant limitations.

## 7. Causal Interpretation

Descriptive associations will not be presented as evidence of causation.

Causal claims will only be considered where the underlying research design supports them and relevant limitations have been assessed.

## 8. Reproducibility

Analysis scripts will be stored in the src directory.

Software dependencies will be documented in requirements.txt.

Processing steps, assumptions and outputs will be recorded.

## 9. Limitations

The available evidence may be incomplete, heterogeneous or unsuitable for statistical pooling.

The project will not perform statistical analysis merely because software makes it possible.

Methods will be selected according to the research question and the quality of available evidence.
