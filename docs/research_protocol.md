
# Research Protocol

## Project Title

The Economics of Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare: Economic Value, Human Trust, Institutional Differences and the Governance of AI-Assisted Decision-Making

## 1. Background and Rationale

Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly being considered for applications across healthcare, including administrative processes, diagnostic support, clinical decision support, patient management and healthcare resource allocation.

Its adoption presents potential opportunities to improve operational efficiency, support professional decision-making and contribute to better healthcare outcomes. However, technological capability alone does not establish economic value or clinical benefit.

The adoption of AI may require substantial investment in infrastructure, software, workforce training, data management, system integration, maintenance and ongoing monitoring. Furthermore, its effects may depend on organisational capacity, professional judgement, patient characteristics and the institutional environment in which it is implemented.

The relationship between human decision-making and automated recommendations is also important. Appropriate trust in AI requires evidence of reliability, transparency, suitability and accountability. Excessive reliance may introduce automation bias, while insufficient trust may prevent potentially beneficial technologies from being used effectively.

This research therefore approaches AI as a technological, economic and institutional intervention. It seeks to understand the conditions under which AI may create value for healthcare systems and the factors that influence its costs, benefits, risks and distributional consequences.

## 2. Central Research Question

Under what economic, institutional and policy conditions can artificial intelligence improve healthcare efficiency and outcomes, how should its costs, benefits and risks be evaluated, and to what extent can healthcare professionals, patients and organisations reasonably trust AI-assisted decisions?

## 3. Research Questions

RQ1: Does healthcare AI generate measurable economic value and improvements in productivity, efficiency and patient outcomes?

RQ2: What direct, indirect, implementation, maintenance and opportunity costs are associated with healthcare AI?

RQ3: How does AI affect professional autonomy, workforce productivity, clinical responsibilities and human decision-making?

RQ4: What technical, human and organisational factors determine the appropriate level of trust in AI-assisted healthcare decisions?

RQ5: Why might the economic and clinical effects of the same AI technology differ across healthcare organisations, systems and population groups?

RQ6: What policy, reimbursement, regulatory and accountability arrangements support safe, equitable and economically sustainable AI adoption?

## 4. Research Aim

To examine the economic, institutional, human and policy implications of AI adoption in healthcare and identify the conditions under which it may contribute to efficient, effective, equitable and sustainable healthcare systems.

## 5. Research Objectives

The research will:

1. Evaluate evidence concerning the economic value and productivity implications of healthcare AI.
2. Identify the full costs associated with its adoption and continued use.
3. Examine effects on workforce responsibilities, professional autonomy and human judgement.
4. Investigate the reliability and appropriate trust of AI-assisted decisions.
5. Analyse institutional differences and the transferability of evidence.
6. Examine policy, reimbursement, regulation and accountability arrangements.

## 6. Geographical Scope

The initial geographical focus will be NHS England.

International evidence will be considered where it provides relevant comparisons or contributes to understanding differences in healthcare financing, organisation, regulation and implementation.

Evidence from other countries will not automatically be treated as directly transferable to NHS England.

## 7. Research Design

This project will use an independent secondary-research design.

The principal methods will include:

- Structured searches of academic and institutional literature.
- Transparent screening and classification of relevant studies.
- Extraction of economic, organisational and clinical findings.
- Comparative synthesis of published evidence.
- Examination of institutional and human factors.
- Illustrative economic modelling using documented assumptions and suitable published inputs.

The research will not involve primary patient data collection, clinical experiments or direct intervention in healthcare services.

## 8. Evidence Sources

Potential sources include:

- PubMed and Europe PMC.
- Google Scholar for supplementary discovery.
- National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE).
- World Health Organization (WHO).
- NHS England.
- UK government publications.
- Office for National Statistics.
- Peer-reviewed health economics and health policy journals.
- Other credible international health institutions.

The eligibility and reuse conditions of each source will be checked individually.

## 9. Inclusion Criteria

Sources may be included where they:

- Address AI applications in healthcare.
- Contribute to at least one research question.
- Have identifiable authors or a credible institutional publisher.
- Provide a clear publication date.
- Explain their methodology or evidence basis.
- Provide sufficiently transparent findings for critical assessment.

Relevant evidence may include peer-reviewed studies, official reports, economic evaluations, implementation studies and credible research reviews.

## 10. Exclusion Criteria

Sources will generally be excluded where they:

- Contain unsupported claims or unverifiable findings.
- Are primarily promotional material without independent evidence.
- Do not address the research questions.
- Have unclear authorship or provenance.
- Contain data whose reuse permissions cannot be established.
- Present speculative projections as observed economic outcomes.

Negative or inconclusive research findings will not be excluded simply because they do not support the expected benefits of AI.

## 11. Economic Evaluation Framework

The research will consider:

- Acquisition and development costs.
- Infrastructure and integration costs.
- Data preparation and management.
- Workforce training and implementation.
- Maintenance and monitoring.
- Professional oversight.
- Potential productivity gains.
- Changes in resource utilisation.
- Patient outcomes and quality of care.
- Opportunity costs.
- Potential costs arising from errors or unintended consequences.

Where appropriate, published evidence may inform budget-impact analysis, cost-consequence analysis or illustrative cost-effectiveness modelling.

The choice of economic method will depend on the availability and quality of evidence.

## 12. Human and Institutional Factors

The research will examine:

- Automation bias and overreliance.
- Under-reliance and barriers to adoption.
- Professional autonomy and human oversight.
- Transparency and explainability.
- Institutional readiness.
- Organisational culture and leadership.
- Workforce skills and training.
- Patient trust and accessibility.
- Accountability and monitoring.

The project will not presume that AI adoption is inherently beneficial or harmful.

## 13. Ethical and Data Protection Principles

The project will prioritise:

- Publicly accessible evidence and appropriately licensed aggregate data.
- Verification of source-specific reuse conditions.
- No identifiable patient records.
- No unnecessary sensitive personal information.
- No recruitment or experiments involving participants.
- No redistribution of restricted datasets.
- Appropriate attribution of all third-party material.
- Transparent reporting of uncertainty and limitations.
- Respectful and non-stigmatising interpretation.

Public availability will not be treated as automatic permission to redistribute or republish data.

## 14. Data Management and Reproducibility

A source register will record publication details, provenance, access conditions and relevance.

Research calculations and analytical procedures will be documented. Original source files will not be uploaded to the public repository unless their licences expressly permit redistribution.

The project will use reproducible Python scripts and documented assumptions where quantitative analysis is appropriate.

## 15. Research Limitations

Potential limitations include:

- Differences in definitions of AI and healthcare productivity.
- Limited availability of comparable economic evaluations.
- Variation in healthcare financing and organisational structures.
- Short follow-up periods in some studies.
- Publication and reporting bias.
- Limited transferability between healthcare systems.
- Uncertainty surrounding long-term costs and outcomes.
- Difficulty attributing observed changes specifically to AI.

The strength of conclusions will reflect the quality and limitations of the available evidence.

## 16. Expected Contribution

The project aims to develop an integrated health economics and policy perspective on healthcare AI.

It will examine not only whether AI can create economic value, but also the institutional, professional and policy conditions required for that value to be realised and sustained.

The intended contribution is an evidence-based framework for evaluating AI adoption while recognising the importance of human judgement, accountability, equity and responsible trust.

## 17. Research Status

This protocol describes the proposed research design. It does not report completed literature searches, empirical results or established conclusions.

The protocol may be updated when justified by evidence, methodological considerations or clearly documented changes in scope.
