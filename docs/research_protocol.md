# Revised research protocol

## Title

Preparing the Healthcare Workforce for Artificial Intelligence: Clinical Accuracy, Human–AI Collaboration, Professional Training and Institutional Readiness.

## Research design

Independent structured literature review and comparative secondary evidence synthesis.

## Central research question

How can healthcare systems prepare existing and future healthcare professionals to work accurately, safely and effectively with artificial intelligence while maintaining human clinical judgement and accountability?

## Geographic focus

NHS England is the primary policy and institutional context. International research is included where relevant, with transferability assessed rather than assumed.

## Evidence themes

1. AI literacy and professional competence.
2. Healthcare AI education and training programmes.
3. Clinical accuracy and human–AI collaboration.
4. Undergraduate, postgraduate and continuing education.
5. Simulation, assessment and supervised practice.
6. Institutional readiness and governance.
7. Patient communication, trust and accountability.
8. Economic and service implications.

## Evidence selection

Relevant peer-reviewed research, systematic reviews, official healthcare guidance, professional education materials and policy documents will be considered.

Sources will be assessed for relevance, methodological quality, date, setting, transparency and applicability to NHS England.

## Synthesis

Evidence will be organised by research question and training theme. Quantitative results will retain their original units and context. Heterogeneous studies will not be combined without methodological justification.

## Case study

Diabetic retinopathy screening will illustrate the clinical implications of AI-assisted screening, diagnostic errors, human review and staff competence.

## Economic analysis

Economic considerations will examine the resources needed for training and safe implementation. Speculative savings will not be presented as observed benefits.

## Ethics and data

The project uses secondary evidence and does not recruit participants, collect new patient data or access confidential organisational records.

## Limitations

The project is constrained by variation in study designs, incomplete reporting, differences between healthcare systems and the limited evidence linking AI education to patient outcomes.

## Reporting

The manuscript will distinguish published findings, the researcher's synthesis and proposed recommendations. It will not claim formal systematic-review registration, NHS endorsement, clinical validation or professional accreditation unless independently established.
