# Reimbursement, Regulation and Governance of Healthcare AI

## 1. Purpose

This document examines the role of reimbursement, regulation and governance in the adoption of healthcare AI.

## 2. Why reimbursement matters

Healthcare organisations need to understand how the costs of an AI technology will be funded.

Relevant considerations include:

- Procurement.
- Software licensing.
- Ongoing maintenance.
- Service budgets.
- Commissioning arrangements.
- Reimbursement.
- Budget impact.
- Long-term financial sustainability.

A technology may demonstrate clinical value without having a clear funding route.

## 3. Evidence requirements

Decision-makers require evidence that is appropriate to the technology's intended purpose and level of risk.

Evidence may include:

- Technical performance.
- Clinical effectiveness.
- Safety.
- Usability.
- Economic value.
- Real-world performance.
- Equity.
- Ongoing monitoring.

## 4. NICE and digital health technologies

The NICE Evidence Standards Framework provides guidance on evidence requirements for digital health technologies.

NICE's approach highlights the importance of describing the technology, its intended use, the relevant care pathway and the evidence supporting its adoption.

Economic evaluation and budget impact are important where the technology has financial consequences for healthcare services.

## 5. Regulatory governance

Healthcare AI must operate within the applicable regulatory framework.

Relevant areas include:

- Intended purpose.
- Medical device requirements where applicable.
- Clinical safety.
- Data protection.
- Cybersecurity.
- Accountability.
- Performance monitoring.
- Incident management.

Regulatory status must be checked for the specific technology and its intended use. It must not be assumed that all AI systems have identical regulatory requirements.

## 6. Organisational accountability

Healthcare providers need clear arrangements for:

- Procurement decisions.
- Clinical oversight.
- System monitoring.
- Staff training.
- Patient communication.
- Error reporting.
- Escalation.
- Responsibility when systems fail.

## 7. Economic implications

Unclear reimbursement can delay implementation or create financial uncertainty.

Ongoing governance and monitoring also require resources that should be considered in economic evaluation.

## 8. Implications for NHS adoption

A sustainable adoption decision should consider clinical evidence, costs, service capacity, regulatory requirements and the practical funding arrangements.

## 9. Conclusion

Reimbursement and governance are important conditions for the sustainable adoption of healthcare AI.

A technology's technical capabilities alone do not establish that it can be implemented safely, funded appropriately or maintained over time.
