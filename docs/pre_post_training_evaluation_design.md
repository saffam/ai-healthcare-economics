# Proposed pre- and post-training evaluation design

## Purpose

To describe how a future healthcare organisation could evaluate whether AI training improves professional competence.

## Proposed design

A future evaluation could measure participants before training, immediately after training and at a later follow-up point.

## Baseline

Assess:
- AI knowledge.
- Understanding of intended use.
- Recognition of false positives and false negatives.
- Ability to recognise uncertainty.
- Ability to challenge incorrect AI outputs.
- Confidence and perceived competence separately.

## Immediate post-training assessment

Use comparable but not identical scenarios to examine changes in knowledge and practical performance.

## Follow-up assessment

Assess retention and whether learning is reflected in approved workplace practice.

## Comparison

Where feasible and ethically appropriate, a future study could compare different educational approaches or use a suitable comparison group.

## Important safeguards

- Do not expose patients to unvalidated training exercises.
- Do not use training scores as evidence of clinical qualification without formal validation.
- Use appropriate consent and ethics review if collecting research data from people.
- Protect participant information.
- Account for differences in prior experience and professional role.

## Interpretation

An observed improvement after training does not automatically prove that training caused the improvement. A stronger evaluation should consider alternative explanations, comparison groups and follow-up.
