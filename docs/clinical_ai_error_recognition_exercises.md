# Clinical AI error-recognition exercises

## Educational purpose

The exercises help learners practise critical evaluation of AI outputs. All cases are fictional and designed for education.

## Exercise 1: False reassurance

A fictional AI screening system classifies an image as negative. Other available clinical information raises concern.

Learner task:
- Identify the conflict.
- Explain why the AI output should not be accepted automatically.
- Identify the appropriate human review or escalation route.

## Exercise 2: False positive

A fictional system flags an image as abnormal, but subsequent expert review identifies no target disease.

Learner task:
- Explain the possible consequences of false positives.
- Identify how unnecessary referral or follow-up could arise.
- Describe appropriate review and communication.

## Exercise 3: Poor-quality input

A fictional system produces a result despite an image being difficult to interpret.

Learner task:
- Identify why input quality matters.
- Explain when an output should be treated as uncertain.
- Apply the approved repeat-imaging or human-review pathway.

## Exercise 4: Conflicting clinical information

An AI recommendation conflicts with other clinical evidence.

Learner task:
- Avoid treating the AI recommendation as decisive.
- Review the available evidence.
- Explain the appropriate professional escalation process.

## Exercise 5: Bias and unequal performance

A fictional audit identifies different performance across patient groups.

Learner task:
- Explain why average accuracy can conceal unequal performance.
- Identify what additional evidence is required.
- Describe how the organisation should escalate and investigate the issue.

## Assessment

Each exercise should assess:
1. Recognition of the problem.
2. Understanding of AI limitations.
3. Independent clinical reasoning.
4. Appropriate human review.
5. Escalation and communication.

## Safety boundary

These exercises are educational examples, not clinical guidance or validated diagnostic examinations. Actual clinical decisions must follow the applicable professional and organisational procedures.
