# Equity and Distributional Effects of Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare

## 1. Purpose

This document examines how AI-assisted healthcare technologies may affect different patient populations and healthcare organisations.

## 2. Meaning of equity

Equity concerns whether people have fair opportunities to access and benefit from healthcare.

An AI system may improve average performance while producing unequal outcomes across population groups.

## 3. Potential sources of unequal performance

Differences may arise from:

- Population representation in development datasets.
- Image quality.
- Clinical characteristics.
- Age.
- Disease prevalence.
- Coexisting health conditions.
- Access to screening.
- Availability of appropriate equipment.

These factors must be evaluated using relevant evidence rather than assumed to affect every system in the same way.

## 4. Access to healthcare

AI may potentially improve access where it increases screening capacity or supports services with limited specialist resources.

However, unequal infrastructure and differences in service availability may also create unequal access to AI-supported screening.

## 5. Distribution of economic benefits

Economic benefits may not be distributed equally.

Some organisations may have the infrastructure and workforce to implement AI efficiently, while others may face higher initial costs.

Patients may also experience different benefits depending on attendance, access, referral and follow-up.

## 6. Risks of unequal outcomes

Potential risks include:

- Differences in diagnostic performance.
- Unequal access to screening.
- Unequal access to follow-up.
- Under-representation in training data.
- Differences in implementation quality.
- Unequal distribution of service resources.

## 7. Governance and fairness

The WHO guidance on ethics and governance of AI for health emphasises human rights, accountability, transparency, inclusion and the need to address risks associated with AI in healthcare.

Equity should therefore be considered throughout design, validation, implementation and monitoring.

## 8. Implications for economic evaluation

Economic evaluation should consider not only total costs and average benefits but also who receives the benefits and who bears the costs.

Where evidence allows, results should be examined across relevant patient groups and organisational settings.

Distributional effects should not be assigned invented monetary values.

## 9. Limitations

Published studies may not provide sufficient subgroup information to assess equity comprehensively.

The absence of reported differences does not establish that outcomes are equitable.

## 10. Conclusion

Equity is an important consideration in the economic and ethical evaluation of healthcare AI.

A technology should not be judged solely by average diagnostic performance or aggregate financial savings.

Its distributional consequences, accessibility and performance across relevant populations must also be considered.
