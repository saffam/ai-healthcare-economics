# Economic Model Design: Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the proposed design of an illustrative economic model for examining the potential costs, benefits and risks of artificial intelligence (AI) in healthcare.

The model will support structured comparison between alternative approaches to healthcare decision-making and service delivery.

It will not initially be presented as a formal health technology assessment or as evidence that a particular AI system is cost-effective.

Its assumptions, data sources, calculations and limitations will be documented transparently.

## 2. Proposed Model Scope

The initial model will use the NHS England context as its principal reference point.

The analysis will focus on the economic implications of introducing AI into a defined healthcare task or pathway.

A specific clinical application will be selected only after the literature review identifies a suitable evidence base.

The model will not combine unrelated AI applications into one set of economic estimates.

## 3. Comparator Scenarios

The proposed model will consider three scenarios.

### Scenario A: Current practice without the AI intervention

This scenario represents the relevant existing approach to the selected healthcare task or pathway.

It will provide the comparator against which the other scenarios are assessed.

### Scenario B: AI-assisted decision-making with human oversight

This scenario considers an AI system that provides information or recommendations while a qualified healthcare professional retains an appropriate role in reviewing and making decisions.

The model will consider system costs, training, review time, workflow changes and potential effects on outcomes.

### Scenario C: More integrated AI-supported workflow with human oversight

This scenario considers a greater level of integration between AI and the relevant healthcare workflow.

It will examine whether additional integration could affect staff time, service capacity, costs, monitoring requirements and risks.

The scenario does not assume that greater integration necessarily produces greater economic value.

The precise boundaries of Scenarios B and C will depend on the selected application and available evidence.

## 4. Perspective

The initial analysis will adopt an NHS or healthcare-provider perspective where the available evidence permits.

This means considering costs and benefits relevant to the healthcare organisation or system.

A broader societal perspective may be discussed separately where evidence is available, but it will not be combined with the provider perspective without clear explanation.

## 5. Time Horizon

The model will specify a time horizon appropriate to the selected application.

It will distinguish between:

- Initial implementation costs.
- Short-term operational effects.
- Recurring annual costs.
- Longer-term outcomes and sustainability.

The time horizon will be justified and recorded once the application and evidence base have been selected.

## 6. Cost Inputs

Potential cost inputs include:

- Acquisition, licensing or development costs.
- Hardware and infrastructure.
- Data preparation and management.
- System integration.
- Staff training.
- Implementation and change management.
- Human review and oversight.
- Maintenance and technical support.
- Monitoring, evaluation and governance.
- Cybersecurity and risk management.
- Opportunity costs.

Each input will be included only where it is relevant to the selected application and supported by evidence or clearly identified assumptions.

## 7. Benefit and Outcome Inputs

Potential measures include:

- Staff time per task.
- Number of tasks completed.
- Service capacity.
- Waiting times.
- Resource utilisation.
- Relevant clinical outcomes.
- Avoided or additional investigations.
- Changes in workflow.
- Patient access and experience.

The model will distinguish between operational measures and patient outcomes.

A reduction in task time will not automatically be treated as a financial saving. The research will consider whether saved time is converted into additional capacity, reduced expenditure or another measurable benefit.

## 8. Risk and Uncertainty

The model will consider relevant uncertainties, including:

- Variation in system performance.
- Changes in staff workload.
- Implementation delays.
- Additional review requirements.
- Errors and associated consequences.
- Changes in technology or maintenance costs.
- Differences between organisations.
- Uncertainty in published estimates.

Risks will be included only where they can be meaningfully described or quantified using defensible evidence.

Where reliable monetary estimates are unavailable, the issue will be discussed qualitatively rather than assigned an invented financial value.

## 9. Evidence and Assumptions Register

Every model input will be documented with:

- Input name.
- Definition and unit.
- Value, if available.
- Source.
- Source year.
- Country and healthcare setting.
- Whether the input is observed, estimated or illustrative.
- Any transformation or calculation applied.
- Relevant limitations.

Illustrative assumptions will be clearly labelled and kept separate from observed evidence.

No input will be presented as an empirical finding unless it is supported by an appropriate source.

## 10. Economic Measures

Depending on the application and evidence available, the model may report:

- Total costs by scenario.
- Incremental costs between scenarios.
- Cost per task.
- Cost per relevant service outcome.
- Changes in staff time.
- Changes in service capacity.
- Budget impact over the selected time horizon.

Cost-effectiveness measures will be used only where suitable outcome data and an appropriate analytical method are available.

## 11. Sensitivity and Scenario Analysis

The analysis will examine how conclusions change when important assumptions vary.

Potential variables include:

- AI acquisition or licensing costs.
- Implementation costs.
- Staff training requirements.
- Human review time.
- System utilisation.
- Changes in productivity.
- Maintenance costs.
- Relevant outcome measures.

The range of values used will be justified using published evidence or clearly labelled illustrative assumptions.

Where evidence is insufficient to establish credible ranges, the limitation will be reported.

## 12. Currency, Price Year and Discounting

All monetary inputs will be recorded with their original currency and price year.

Where conversion or inflation adjustment is necessary, the method and source will be documented.

If the time horizon and analytical requirements justify discounting, the discount rate and its justification will be stated explicitly.

No monetary values from different years or currencies will be combined without appropriate adjustment or explanation.

## 13. Interpretation

The model will be used to explore the conditions under which AI may create economic value.

It will not be used to claim that AI is universally cost-saving, cost-effective or clinically beneficial.

Results will be interpreted in light of:

- Evidence quality.
- Model assumptions.
- Institutional context.
- Human oversight requirements.
- Patient outcomes.
- Distributional effects.
- Uncertainty.

## 14. Limitations

The model will depend on the quality and availability of published evidence.

Some costs, benefits and risks may be difficult to quantify. Organisational differences may also limit the transferability of estimates.

The model's illustrative nature will be clearly stated wherever applicable.

## 15. Relationship to the Research Questions

The model is intended to bring together evidence relevant to RQ1–RQ6, with particular emphasis on economic value, costs, institutional context and policy conditions.
