
# Healthcare Productivity and Artificial Intelligence

## 1. Research Purpose

This document establishes a framework for investigating the relationship between artificial intelligence adoption and productivity in healthcare.

Productivity will be examined in relation to the resources used to deliver healthcare services and the quantity and quality of outputs produced.

## 2. Main Areas of Investigation

### 2.1 Time Efficiency

- Does AI reduce the time required to complete particular tasks?
- Is time saved by one professional transferred to another?
- Does additional verification work offset the time saved?

### 2.2 Service Capacity

- Does AI enable healthcare organisations to serve more patients?
- Does it reduce operational bottlenecks?
- Does it change waiting times?

### 2.3 Quality and Outcomes

- Are services delivered with comparable or improved quality?
- Are errors reduced or introduced?
- Are patient outcomes affected?

### 2.4 Resource Utilisation

- Does AI improve the use of staff, equipment and infrastructure?
- Does it reduce unnecessary resource consumption?
- Does it introduce additional operational requirements?

### 2.5 Organisational Productivity

- Does the technology improve overall organisational performance?
- Are improvements sustained over time?
- Are implementation and maintenance costs considered?

## 3. Conceptual Productivity Framework

Productivity may be considered as:

Productivity = Healthcare outputs / Resources used

However, healthcare outputs are multidimensional and cannot always be represented by the number of patients treated or tasks completed.

Quality, safety and patient outcomes must also be considered.

## 4. Human Contribution

The analysis will examine whether AI:

- Complements professional expertise.
- Automates selected tasks.
- Changes professional responsibilities.
- Introduces additional checking requirements.
- Changes the allocation of human resources.

## 5. Measurement and Evidence

Productivity claims will be assessed using published evidence.

Where possible, the analysis will distinguish:

- Task-level time savings.
- Staff-level productivity.
- Department-level productivity.
- Organisation-level productivity.
- Wider healthcare system effects.

## 6. Reporting Principle

An improvement in task speed will not automatically be interpreted as an improvement in healthcare productivity.

Conclusions will account for service quality, resource use, implementation costs and unintended consequences.
