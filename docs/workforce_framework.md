
# Healthcare Workforce and Artificial Intelligence

## 1. Research Purpose

This document establishes a framework for examining how AI adoption may influence healthcare employment, professional responsibilities, workforce productivity and the organisation of healthcare work.

The analysis will distinguish between task automation, task augmentation and changes in professional responsibilities.

## 2. Task Automation

Task automation refers to the use of technology to perform particular tasks that may previously have required human input.

The research will examine:

- Which healthcare tasks may be automated.
- Whether automation reduces workload.
- Whether new monitoring or verification tasks are created.
- Whether the technology changes staffing requirements.

## 3. Task Augmentation

Task augmentation refers to AI supporting human professionals in performing their existing responsibilities.

Potential areas include:

- Information retrieval.
- Clinical decision support.
- Administrative assistance.
- Diagnostic support.
- Planning and resource allocation.

The analysis will consider whether AI improves professional capabilities and under what conditions.

## 4. Professional Autonomy

The research will examine:

- Whether professionals retain meaningful decision-making authority.
- How automated recommendations influence professional judgement.
- Whether appropriate opportunities for review and override exist.
- How responsibilities are distributed between professionals and organisations.

## 5. Automation Bias and Overreliance

Automation bias may occur when individuals place excessive reliance on automated recommendations.

The analysis will investigate:

- Whether professionals accept AI recommendations without sufficient review.
- Whether contradictory evidence may be overlooked.
- How workload and time pressure influence reliance.
- Whether training and system design support appropriate scrutiny.

## 6. Under-reliance

The research will also consider situations where professionals may not use AI recommendations despite potentially relevant evidence.

Potential factors include:

- Limited confidence in system reliability.
- Poor workflow integration.
- Insufficient training.
- Lack of transparency.
- Concerns about accountability.

## 7. Workforce Skills and Training

The research will examine:

- Digital literacy.
- AI-related training.
- Continuing professional development.
- Technical support requirements.
- Changes in professional competencies.

## 8. Employment and Labour Market Implications

Potential areas of investigation include:

- Changes in task composition.
- Demand for technical and clinical skills.
- Workforce substitution and augmentation.
- Changes in staffing patterns.
- Distribution of productivity gains.

Employment effects will not be assumed and will require supporting evidence.

## 9. Organisational Responsibility

Healthcare organisations may need to establish:

- Appropriate human oversight.
- Clear decision-making responsibilities.
- Staff training.
- Monitoring and reporting processes.
- Procedures for reviewing errors and concerns.

## 10. Reporting Principle

The research will not frame AI as inherently replacing or benefiting healthcare professionals.

Its workforce implications will be evaluated according to the available evidence, institutional context and nature of the tasks involved.
