
# Economic Cost Framework for Healthcare AI

## 1. Purpose

This framework identifies the economic costs that may arise when artificial intelligence technologies are developed, procured, implemented and maintained in healthcare organisations.

The objective is to avoid evaluating AI solely on the basis of its initial purchase or development cost.

## 2. Cost Categories

### 2.1 Acquisition and Development Costs

- Software procurement.
- Licensing fees.
- Technology development.
- Vendor contracts.
- Initial implementation expenditure.

### 2.2 Infrastructure Costs

- Computing infrastructure.
- Hardware.
- Storage.
- Network capacity.
- System upgrades.

### 2.3 Data Costs

- Data preparation.
- Data cleaning.
- Data integration.
- Data governance.
- Data quality management.

### 2.4 Workforce Costs

- Staff training.
- Technical personnel.
- Clinical oversight.
- Additional administrative responsibilities.
- Changes to staffing requirements.

### 2.5 Integration Costs

- Integration with existing healthcare systems.
- Workflow redesign.
- Interoperability.
- Testing and validation.
- Implementation support.

### 2.6 Ongoing Operational Costs

- Software maintenance.
- Model monitoring.
- Performance evaluation.
- Cybersecurity.
- Technical support.
- Model updates and revalidation.

### 2.7 Risk-Related Costs

- Costs associated with errors.
- Additional investigations.
- Delayed or inappropriate decisions.
- Corrective procedures.
- Incident management.
- Legal and regulatory compliance.

These costs should be included only where relevant and supported by evidence.

## 3. Opportunity Cost

Opportunity cost refers to the value of the alternative use of resources allocated to AI adoption.

For example, expenditure on AI infrastructure may compete with other investments such as staffing, medical equipment, training or service improvements.

## 4. Economic Perspective

The analysis will distinguish between:

- Healthcare provider perspective.
- Healthcare payer perspective.
- Wider health system perspective.
- Societal perspective, where suitable evidence exists.

Costs may differ depending on the perspective adopted.

## 5. Time Horizon

The research will consider initial implementation costs and ongoing costs over the period examined by each study.

Long-term costs will not be assumed where evidence is unavailable.

## 6. Reporting Principle

All cost estimates must identify their source, currency, price year, geographical setting and relevant assumptions.

Costs from different years or countries will not be compared without appropriate consideration of inflation, purchasing power and differences in healthcare systems.
