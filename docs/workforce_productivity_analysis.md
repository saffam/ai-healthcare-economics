# Workforce Productivity and Professional Responsibilities in Healthcare AI

## 1. Purpose

This document examines how healthcare AI may affect workforce productivity, staff responsibilities and the organisation of clinical work.

## 2. Potential productivity effects

AI may influence:

- Time spent reviewing clinical information.
- Allocation of routine tasks.
- Prioritisation of cases.
- Administrative workload.
- Clinical decision-making.
- Demand for specialist oversight.

These effects depend on the technology and the implementation setting.

## 3. Time saved versus productivity

Time saved is not automatically equivalent to increased productivity.

Productivity improvement requires consideration of how released capacity is used.

For example, reduced image-review time may enable additional patients to be screened, reduce waiting times or allow staff to undertake other clinical activities.

If staff capacity is not redeployed, the time saving may not produce a measurable increase in service output.

## 4. Workforce costs

Relevant economic costs include:

- Initial training.
- Continuing professional development.
- Staff time.
- Clinical supervision.
- Technical support.
- Additional quality assurance.
- Changes in staffing requirements.

## 5. Professional responsibilities

AI may alter the distribution of professional responsibilities.

Human professionals may remain responsible for interpreting uncertain findings, managing exceptions, communicating with patients and ensuring appropriate follow-up.

The introduction of AI should not be interpreted as eliminating professional judgement.

## 6. Risks

Potential risks include:

- Automation bias.
- Overreliance on system outputs.
- Deskilling.
- Increased workload from exception handling.
- Unclear accountability.
- Insufficient training.

These are risks to evaluate, not outcomes that can be assumed to occur in every implementation.

## 7. Evidence and interpretation

The trust review by Tun et al. (2025) identifies professional autonomy, training, usability, reliability and transparency as relevant considerations in healthcare workers' experiences of AI-based clinical decision support.

## 8. Implications for the NHS case study

The economic model should include staff time and clinical oversight where supported by evidence.

It should distinguish cash-releasing savings from released capacity and non-financial productivity improvements.

## 9. Conclusion

The workforce consequences of healthcare AI are central to its economic evaluation.

A credible analysis must examine how AI changes work, whether capacity is redeployed and whether new responsibilities create additional resource requirements.
