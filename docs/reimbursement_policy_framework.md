# Reimbursement, Incentives and Policy Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework examines how healthcare financing, reimbursement, procurement and policy arrangements may influence the adoption and economic sustainability of artificial intelligence (AI) in healthcare.

The research will consider whether the costs and benefits of AI are experienced by the same organisations and stakeholders, and whether existing incentives support appropriate implementation.

## 2. Healthcare Financing and Reimbursement

The analysis will consider how healthcare organisations pay for, fund or receive reimbursement for AI-related services.

Relevant questions include:

- Who pays for the AI system?
- Who is responsible for implementation and maintenance?
- Which organisation receives any financial savings?
- Who benefits from improved service capacity?
- Are additional costs recognised within existing funding arrangements?
- Are there incentives to adopt, maintain or discontinue a system?

The research will not assume that a particular reimbursement model applies uniformly across the NHS or internationally.

## 3. Financial and Organisational Incentives

Potential incentive-related issues include:

- Differences between organisational budgets and system-wide benefits.
- Whether savings can be retained by the organisation making the investment.
- Whether payment arrangements reward service volume, quality or outcomes.
- Whether procurement decisions adequately consider lifetime costs.
- Whether short-term budget pressures discourage investments with longer-term benefits.

These issues will be examined using documented policy and economic evidence.

## 4. Economic Evaluation and Budget Impact

The research will distinguish between:

### 4.1 Cost-effectiveness

Cost-effectiveness analysis examines the relationship between costs and relevant outcomes.

The appropriate outcome measure will depend on the AI application and the available evidence.

### 4.2 Budget impact

Budget impact analysis examines the expected financial consequences of adopting an intervention for a particular organisation or healthcare budget over a specified period.

An intervention may be cost-effective over the longer term while still requiring substantial initial expenditure.

### 4.3 Affordability and financial sustainability

Affordability concerns whether an organisation or healthcare system can meet the financial commitments associated with adoption.

Financial sustainability also requires consideration of ongoing costs, replacement, maintenance, monitoring and future changes in technology.

## 5. Procurement and Implementation Decisions

The research will consider:

- Procurement and licensing arrangements.
- Contract duration and renewal.
- Data access and portability.
- Integration costs.
- Supplier support and maintenance.
- Performance monitoring.
- Exit arrangements and switching costs.
- Risks of dependence on a particular supplier.

The project will not assume that the lowest initial purchase price represents the lowest total cost.

## 6. Governance and Accountability

Relevant policy considerations include:

- Regulatory requirements.
- Clinical and organisational responsibility.
- Data protection and information governance.
- Safety monitoring.
- Procedures for reporting and responding to incidents.
- Requirements for evidence of effectiveness.
- Transparency about system limitations.

The analysis will distinguish between legal requirements, policy guidance and recommended good practice.

## 7. NHS England Context

The initial analysis will consider relevant NHS England policy, procurement, commissioning and funding arrangements.

International examples may be included where they provide useful comparisons, but differences in financing and institutional arrangements will be made explicit.

## 8. Analytical Principles

The research will:

1. Distinguish cost-effectiveness from affordability.
2. Consider the perspective of the organisation paying for AI.
3. Identify where costs and benefits may fall on different stakeholders.
4. Consider both initial and recurring expenditure.
5. Avoid assuming that adoption is financially beneficial simply because a technology improves a clinical or operational measure.
6. Examine whether incentives support safe and sustainable use.
7. Distinguish established policy from proposals or recommendations.

## 9. Limitations

Publicly available information may not reveal confidential contract prices, commercial arrangements or detailed organisational budgets.

Where such information is unavailable, the project will identify the evidence gap rather than estimate undisclosed costs without a defensible basis.
