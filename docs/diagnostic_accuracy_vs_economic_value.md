# Diagnostic Accuracy Versus Economic Value in Healthcare AI

## 1. Purpose

This analysis examines why improved diagnostic accuracy does not necessarily produce improved economic value.

## 2. Accuracy is not cost-effectiveness

Diagnostic accuracy describes how well a test identifies a target condition.

Economic evaluation considers both the resources used and the consequences produced by an intervention.

An AI system can demonstrate high sensitivity or specificity while still requiring expensive infrastructure, licensing, maintenance and clinical oversight.

## 3. Costs and consequences

Economic value depends on several factors:

- Acquisition and licensing costs.
- Implementation expenditure.
- Staff time.
- Training.
- Maintenance.
- Diagnostic errors.
- Referral activity.
- Clinical outcomes.
- Patient follow-up.
- The effectiveness of the existing comparator.

## 4. Importance of the comparator

An AI system should be evaluated against a clearly defined alternative.

A system may outperform an existing diagnostic test but still fail to provide sufficient additional health benefit to justify its additional costs.

Conversely, a system with similar diagnostic accuracy could potentially provide value if it reduces resource use without worsening important outcomes.

## 5. Evidence from diabetic retinopathy screening

Wang et al. (2024) examined economic evaluation of medical AI in a diabetic retinopathy screening case study.

Their analysis illustrates that the most accurate configuration does not necessarily produce the most cost-effective outcome.

This is an important reason to distinguish diagnostic performance from economic evaluation.

## 6. Incremental economic evaluation

Where appropriate data are available, the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio can be expressed as:

ICER = Difference in costs / Difference in health outcomes

The ratio must be interpreted alongside the underlying costs, outcomes, uncertainty and relevant decision threshold.

It should not be calculated when valid incremental outcomes are unavailable.

## 7. Implications for NHS decision-making

NHS adoption decisions require evidence concerning clinical effectiveness, service requirements, implementation costs, budget impact and relevant patient outcomes.

The NICE Evidence Standards Framework provides a structured approach to evaluating digital health technologies.

## 8. Limitations

Published studies may use different outcome measures, perspectives, comparators and time horizons.

This limits direct comparison between their estimates.

## 9. Conclusion

Diagnostic accuracy is an important component of healthcare AI evaluation, but it is not a substitute for economic evaluation.

The economic value of AI depends on whether improvements in the healthcare pathway justify the additional resources required and produce meaningful outcomes.
