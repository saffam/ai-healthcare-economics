# Research Question and Evidence Mapping

## Purpose

This document connects the six research questions to the project's evidence base, analytical methods and remaining evidence requirements.

The mapping is intended to prevent the final report from making conclusions that exceed the evidence available.

## RQ1 — Economic value

**Question:** Does healthcare AI generate measurable economic value and improvements in productivity, efficiency and patient outcomes?

Relevant evidence:
- Wu et al. (2025), systematic review of economic evaluations.
- El Arab and Al Moosa (2025), systematic review of cost-effectiveness and budget impact.
- Wang et al. (2024), diabetic retinopathy economic evaluation.
- Leigh et al. (2025), review of economic evaluations in diabetic retinopathy.
- Diagnostic accuracy and clinical outcome studies.

Analytical approach:
- Distinguish cost-effectiveness, cost-saving, budget impact and productivity.
- Examine clinical outcomes separately from technical performance.
- Assess whether reported economic benefits are observed or modelled.
- Consider the time horizon and economic perspective.

Evidence gap:
The current evidence register does not yet contain a fully verified, NHS-specific estimate of the incremental economic value of AI-assisted diabetic retinopathy screening.

## RQ2 — Costs

**Question:** What direct, indirect, implementation, maintenance and opportunity costs are associated with healthcare AI?

Relevant evidence:
- NICE Evidence Standards Framework.
- NHS National Cost Collection.
- Published healthcare AI economic evaluations.
- Diabetic retinopathy screening pathway documentation.

Analytical approach:
- Identify acquisition, infrastructure, integration, training, staffing, maintenance and monitoring costs.
- Distinguish one-off and recurring expenditure.
- Consider additional costs caused by diagnostic errors and pathway changes.
- Identify costs that are unavailable rather than treating them as zero.

Evidence gap:
A complete and verified NHS-specific set of AI acquisition, integration, staffing and maintenance costs has not been established.

## RQ3 — Workforce and human judgement

**Question:** How does AI affect professional autonomy, workforce productivity, clinical responsibilities and human decision-making?

Relevant evidence:
- Tun et al. (2025), systematic review of healthcare worker trust.
- Published studies of AI-assisted and autonomous screening.
- NHS pathway and professional oversight requirements.

Analytical approach:
- Distinguish assisted from autonomous systems.
- Examine task redistribution and changes in professional responsibilities.
- Distinguish time saved from realised productivity.
- Consider the implications of human review and escalation.

Evidence gap:
The available preliminary evidence does not establish a generalisable NHS estimate of net workforce time saved or financial savings.

## RQ4 — Trust

**Question:** What technical, human and organisational factors determine appropriate trust in AI-assisted healthcare decisions?

Relevant evidence:
- Tun et al. (2025).
- World Health Organization (2021) guidance.
- Published clinical validation and implementation research.

Analytical approach:
- Examine reliability, transparency, usability and validation.
- Consider training, professional autonomy and accountability.
- Distinguish appropriate reliance from uncritical acceptance.
- Consider patient communication and human oversight.

Evidence gap:
The current project does not contain original patient or healthcare worker trust data. Conclusions must remain based on published evidence.

## RQ5 — Institutional differences

**Question:** Why might the economic and clinical effects of the same AI technology differ across healthcare organisations, systems and population groups?

Relevant evidence:
- Powis et al. (2026), implementation research framework.
- International economic evaluations.
- NHS diabetic eye screening pathway documentation.
- Published evidence on infrastructure and implementation.

Analytical approach:
- Compare infrastructure, staffing, patient populations and service pathways.
- Examine differences in procurement, integration and maintenance.
- Assess transferability of international findings to NHS England.
- Consider organisational readiness and service capacity.

Evidence gap:
A direct comparative dataset of NHS organisations using the same AI technology has not been established.

## RQ6 — Policy and governance

**Question:** What policy, reimbursement, regulatory and accountability arrangements support safe, equitable and economically sustainable AI adoption?

Relevant evidence:
- World Health Organization (2021).
- NICE Evidence Standards Framework.
- NHS pathway and governance documentation.
- Published implementation research.

Analytical approach:
- Examine evidence requirements, accountability and oversight.
- Consider reimbursement and procurement incentives.
- Assess safety, equity and transparency requirements.
- Distinguish international ethical guidance from applicable UK regulatory requirements.

Evidence gap:
This project is an independent research exercise, not a legal assessment or regulatory compliance audit.

## Overall evidence position

The project has a preliminary evidence base across all six research questions. However, the strength of evidence differs between topics.

Economic evaluations, policy frameworks and systematic reviews provide useful contextual evidence. NHS-specific cost inputs, workforce effects, long-term outcomes and distributional effects remain less complete.

The final report must make these differences visible. It must not imply that all six questions have been answered with equal certainty.

## Reporting rule

Each major conclusion in the final report should be traceable to:
1. A specific source or clearly identified analytical assumption.
2. A relevant research question.
3. A description of the evidence type.
4. An explanation of the limitations.
5. A clear indication of whether the conclusion is observed, modelled or interpretive.
