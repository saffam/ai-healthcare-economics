# Research propositions

This is an exploratory secondary research project. The following propositions guide the evidence synthesis; they are not statistical hypotheses being tested using newly collected participant data.

1. General awareness training alone is unlikely to prepare every professional for the safe use of specialist clinical AI systems.
2. Training should reflect the professional's role, level of interaction with AI and responsibility for clinical decisions.
3. Simulation involving deliberately incorrect or uncertain AI outputs may help professionals practise critical evaluation.
4. Competency assessment should examine demonstrated ability, not course attendance or self-reported confidence alone.
5. Human oversight is meaningful only when professionals have sufficient knowledge, time, authority and access to relevant information to challenge AI outputs.
6. Continuing education and performance monitoring are necessary because AI systems, clinical pathways and evidence can change.
7. Training programmes should be evaluated beyond immediate learner satisfaction, including workplace behaviour and organisational or patient outcomes where evidence permits.
8. The value of workforce preparation should be assessed in terms of safety, quality and appropriate use, not merely financial savings.
