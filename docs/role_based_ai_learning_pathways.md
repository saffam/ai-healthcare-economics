# Role-based AI learning pathways

## Principle

Healthcare professionals should receive education proportionate to their role, the AI systems they encounter and the consequences of acting on AI outputs.

## Pathway A: Professionals who encounter AI outputs

Examples include staff who receive AI-generated documentation, triage information or administrative outputs.

Learning priorities:
- Foundational AI literacy.
- Understanding intended use.
- Recognising obvious errors.
- Data protection and confidentiality.
- Knowing when to seek assistance.

## Pathway B: Professionals who interpret clinical AI outputs

Examples include clinicians who review AI-supported imaging, screening or decision-support results.

Learning priorities:
- System-specific training.
- Diagnostic performance and uncertainty.
- False positives and false negatives.
- Clinical context and independent judgement.
- Escalation and human review.
- Patient communication.

## Pathway C: Professionals who oversee AI implementation

Examples include clinical AI leads, digital health leaders, clinical educators and designated safety specialists.

Learning priorities:
- Clinical validation and evidence appraisal.
- Procurement and implementation considerations.
- Bias, equity and performance monitoring.
- Incident investigation.
- Workforce training and competency assessment.
- Governance and change management.

## Pathway D: Future healthcare professionals

Examples include medical, nursing and allied health students.

Learning priorities:
- Foundational AI literacy.
- Critical thinking and clinical reasoning.
- AI-supported simulation.
- Ethics and professional accountability.
- Appropriate use of AI in supervised learning.

## Boundary

These are proposed learning pathways, not formal professional qualifications or permission to perform activities outside existing professional scope.
