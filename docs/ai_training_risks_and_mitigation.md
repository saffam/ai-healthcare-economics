# Risks and mitigation in healthcare AI training

## Risk 1: False confidence

Learners may become confident without demonstrating competence.

**Mitigation:** Use observed assessments and compare confidence with performance.

## Risk 2: Over-reliance on AI

Staff may accept AI outputs without sufficient independent review.

**Mitigation:** Include incorrect-output scenarios and teach appropriate challenge.

## Risk 3: Deskilling

Excessive reliance on automation may reduce opportunities to practise underlying clinical skills.

**Mitigation:** Preserve foundational clinical education and appropriate independent reasoning.

## Risk 4: Training overload

Additional courses may increase pressure on already busy healthcare staff.

**Mitigation:** Provide protected learning time, short modular content and role-based priorities.

## Risk 5: Unequal training access

Some professional groups or departments may receive fewer learning opportunities.

**Mitigation:** Map workforce needs and monitor access across roles and locations.

## Risk 6: Outdated training

Training may no longer match a changed AI system or clinical pathway.

**Mitigation:** Define update-triggered learning and reassessment.

## Risk 7: Confusion about accountability

Staff may incorrectly assume that responsibility transfers to the AI system.

**Mitigation:** Teach the applicable professional standards and organisational procedures.

## Risk 8: Inadequate organisational support

Staff may be trained but lack the time, authority or support to challenge AI outputs.

**Mitigation:** Make readiness and escalation procedures part of implementation planning.

## Overall conclusion

Training is necessary but insufficient by itself. It must be supported by safe technology, clear responsibilities and organisational capacity.
