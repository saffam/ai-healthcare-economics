# Source Verification Method

## Purpose

This procedure defines how sources are checked before their findings are used in the research manuscript or economic model.

## Verification sequence

1. Search for the original publication or official issuing organisation.
2. Match the title, authors, publication year and journal or organisation.
3. Confirm the DOI or official URL.
4. Obtain the full text where legally and freely available.
5. Identify the study design, population, intervention, comparator and outcomes.
6. Extract numerical findings with units and relevant uncertainty measures.
7. Record the page, table, figure or section supporting each extracted result.
8. Record limitations, conflicts of interest and funding where reported.
9. Assess relevance to the NHS England context.
10. Assign an evidence status.

## Evidence statuses

- **Bibliography verified:** Publication details checked; findings not yet extracted.
- **Full text checked:** Full text inspected; relevant results not necessarily extracted.
- **Extracted:** Relevant findings transcribed with traceable locations.
- **Appraised:** Design, bias risks and limitations assessed.
- **NHS applicability assessed:** Transferability to NHS England explicitly considered.
- **Unverified:** Source or claim could not be adequately checked.
- **Excluded:** Source does not meet the project's criteria, with reason documented.

## Numerical extraction rules

Every numerical estimate must include:

- Value and unit.
- Population and setting.
- Outcome definition.
- Study design.
- Time period.
- Source location within the publication.
- Whether the value is observed, modelled or assumed.

Do not infer missing values from abstracts, secondary summaries or unrelated studies.

## Transferability

International evidence may inform the conceptual model, but its numerical inputs must not be treated as NHS values without a defensible transferability assessment.

Differences in screening pathways, population prevalence, follow-up, staffing, costs, technology configuration and health-system organisation must be considered.

## Reporting

The manuscript must distinguish verified evidence, interpretation, projections and illustrative assumptions. Unresolved evidence gaps must be reported rather than silently filled.
