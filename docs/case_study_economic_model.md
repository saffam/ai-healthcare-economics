# Economic Model Design: AI-Assisted Diabetic Retinopathy Screening

## 1. Objective

The model will examine the potential economic consequences of introducing AI-assisted diabetic retinopathy screening compared with conventional human grading.

The purpose is to identify the costs, outcomes, assumptions and uncertainties that determine economic value.

The model will not assume that AI is cost-saving or cost-effective.

## 2. Proposed comparison

### Strategy A: Conventional screening

Retinal images are assessed through the existing human grading pathway.

### Strategy B: AI-assisted screening

Retinal images are assessed using an AI system. The exact pathway will depend on the evidence for the selected system, including whether human review is retained for positive or uncertain results.

The two pathways must be described consistently before any numerical comparison is undertaken.

## 3. Proposed population

The target population is people with diabetes who are eligible for diabetic retinopathy screening.

Population size and screening frequency must be obtained from reliable published sources before being entered into the model.

## 4. Proposed outcomes

The model should consider:

- Screening costs.
- Number of images assessed.
- Number of people screened.
- Positive and negative screening classifications.
- False-positive and false-negative classifications.
- Referral requirements.
- Follow-up attendance.
- Staff time and resource use.
- Quality-adjusted life-years, where sufficiently supported.
- Total costs and incremental costs.

## 5. Cost categories

### Technology costs

- Software licence or subscription.
- Hardware and equipment.
- Installation and integration.
- Software updates.
- Maintenance and technical support.
- Cybersecurity and information governance.

### Workforce costs

- Image acquisition.
- Human grading.
- Clinical review.
- Training.
- Quality assurance.
- Technical support.
- Management and administration.

### Healthcare pathway costs

- Further diagnostic assessment.
- Specialist appointments.
- Treatment.
- Follow-up.
- Management of complications.

### Wider costs

Where evidence permits:

- Patient travel.
- Patient time.
- Lost income.
- Informal care.

## 6. Economic perspective

The main proposed perspective is the NHS healthcare-system perspective.

A societal perspective may be examined separately if adequate evidence is available.

## 7. Time horizon

The time horizon will be selected after examining the available evidence and the natural history of diabetic retinopathy.

A short-term budget impact analysis and a longer-term cost-utility analysis may be presented separately if the available data support both.

## 8. Model structure

The model will initially use a transparent screening-pathway framework.

The screening process will distinguish:

1. Eligible population.
2. Attendance for screening.
3. Image acquisition.
4. AI or human grading.
5. Screening classification.
6. Referral or no referral.
7. Follow-up attendance.
8. Diagnosis and treatment, where applicable.
9. Longer-term health outcomes, where evidence supports their estimation.

## 9. Uncertainty

Uncertainty will be examined through:

- Alternative technology costs.
- Alternative diagnostic performance.
- Alternative screening volumes.
- Alternative attendance and follow-up rates.
- Alternative staffing assumptions.
- Alternative implementation costs.
- Alternative time horizons.

Scenario values must be supported by evidence or clearly labelled as illustrative assumptions.

## 10. Model limitations

The model may be limited by unavailable NHS-specific data, differences between international healthcare systems, uncertainty in long-term clinical outcomes and incomplete reporting of implementation costs.

No numerical result will be reported as an empirical finding unless its inputs and calculation are documented.

## 11. Reporting

Results should distinguish:

- Total costs.
- Incremental costs.
- Health outcomes.
- Cost per outcome, where appropriate.
- Budget impact.
- Capacity changes.
- Uncertainty.
- Distributional implications.

The analysis will be informed by the NICE Evidence Standards Framework for Digital Health Technologies.
