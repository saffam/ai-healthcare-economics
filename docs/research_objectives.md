
# Research Objectives

## Project Title

The Economics of Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare: Economic Value, Human Trust, Institutional Differences and the Governance of AI-Assisted Decision-Making

## 1. Overall Research Aim

The overarching aim of this research is to critically examine the economic, institutional, human and policy implications of artificial intelligence (AI) adoption in healthcare.

The study seeks to understand the circumstances under which AI can contribute to improvements in healthcare efficiency, productivity and patient outcomes, while considering the financial resources required, the influence of human decision-making, differences in organisational contexts and the governance arrangements necessary for responsible implementation.

Rather than assuming that technological adoption automatically generates economic or clinical benefits, the research aims to develop an evidence-based understanding of the conditions that determine the value, limitations and sustainability of AI in healthcare systems.

## 2. Specific Research Objectives

### Objective 1: Evaluate the Economic Value of Healthcare AI

To examine the available empirical evidence on the contribution of AI to healthcare productivity, operational efficiency, resource allocation and patient outcomes.

This objective will distinguish between technological performance, operational improvements and measurable economic or clinical benefits.

**Linked research question:** RQ1.

### Objective 2: Assess the Full Economic Costs of AI Adoption

To identify and evaluate the direct and indirect costs associated with the development, procurement, implementation, integration, maintenance and monitoring of AI technologies in healthcare.

The analysis will also consider opportunity costs, financial uncertainty and the long-term sustainability of AI investments.

**Linked research question:** RQ2.

### Objective 3: Examine Workforce and Human Decision-Making Implications

To investigate how AI integration influences healthcare workforce productivity, professional responsibilities, clinical autonomy and the interaction between human judgement and automated recommendations.

Particular attention will be given to automation bias, appropriate human oversight and the extent to which AI complements or substitutes for existing professional tasks.

**Linked research question:** RQ3.

### Objective 4: Investigate Trust and the Reliability of AI-Assisted Decisions

To identify the technical, human and organisational factors that influence appropriate trust in AI-assisted healthcare decisions.

The study will examine the importance of validation, transparency, explainability, professional competence, accountability and ongoing monitoring in supporting responsible reliance on AI systems.

**Linked research question:** RQ4.

### Objective 5: Analyse Institutional Differences and Implementation Contexts

To examine how differences in healthcare infrastructure, financial resources, organisational capacity, workforce capabilities and patient populations influence the economic and clinical consequences of AI adoption.

This objective will assess the transferability of research findings across healthcare organisations and systems.

**Linked research question:** RQ5.

### Objective 6: Examine Policy, Governance and Accountability

To evaluate the policy, regulatory, reimbursement and institutional arrangements that can support safe, equitable and economically sustainable AI adoption.

The study will consider how healthcare systems can assess AI technologies, allocate responsibility, monitor performance and ensure that economic and clinical benefits are consistent with wider public health objectives.

**Linked research question:** RQ6.

## 3. Expected Research Contribution

The research aims to contribute an integrated health economics and policy perspective on artificial intelligence in healthcare.

By bringing together economic evaluation, workforce considerations, human trust, institutional context and governance, the study seeks to move beyond technology-centred assessments of AI adoption.

Its intended contribution is a structured framework for understanding the conditions under which AI may generate meaningful healthcare value, the circumstances in which its benefits may be limited, and the safeguards required to support responsible implementation.

## 5. Research Boundaries

The study will be conducted as independent secondary research. It will use published academic literature, publicly available official aggregate information and appropriately licensed secondary data.

It will not involve the collection of identifiable patient information, direct recruitment of participants, clinical experimentation or the deployment of AI systems in healthcare settings.

Any quantitative modelling will clearly distinguish observed evidence from assumptions and illustrative scenarios.

The research will not presume that AI adoption is inherently beneficial or harmful. Conclusions will be based on the quality, relevance and limitations of the available evidence.
