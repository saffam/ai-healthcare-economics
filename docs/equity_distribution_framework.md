# Equity and Distribution of Costs and Benefits Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework examines how the costs, benefits and risks of artificial intelligence (AI) in healthcare may be distributed across patient populations, healthcare organisations and geographical areas.

The research recognises that an intervention may improve average outcomes while producing different effects across groups or settings.

The project will therefore consider not only whether AI creates value, but also who receives that value and who may experience additional costs or risks.

## 2. Dimensions of Equity

### 2.1 Access to healthcare

The research will consider whether AI affects:

- Access to healthcare services.
- Waiting times and service availability.
- Access to specialist assessment.
- Availability of services in different geographical areas.
- Access for people who experience barriers to digital services.

### 2.2 Representation and data quality

AI systems are developed and evaluated using data that may not represent every population equally.

The research will examine published evidence concerning:

- Representativeness of training and evaluation data.
- Differences in system performance across populations.
- Missing or incomplete data.
- Potentially unequal error rates.
- Limitations in external validation.

The project will not assume that a performance difference exists without evidence.

### 2.3 Digital inclusion and accessibility

Relevant factors may include:

- Access to suitable devices and connectivity.
- Digital literacy.
- Language accessibility.
- Disability access requirements.
- Availability of non-digital alternatives.
- Ability to understand and engage with AI-supported services.

### 2.4 Distribution across organisations and regions

The research will consider whether organisations with greater financial, technical or workforce resources are better positioned to adopt and benefit from AI.

It will also consider whether implementation costs or infrastructure requirements may create additional disadvantages for resource-constrained settings.

## 3. Distribution of Economic Costs and Benefits

The analysis will distinguish between:

- Costs borne by healthcare organisations.
- Costs borne by healthcare systems or public budgets.
- Time and workload implications for healthcare professionals.
- Benefits experienced by patients.
- Benefits experienced by providers.
- Benefits experienced by the wider healthcare system.

An overall economic benefit does not necessarily mean that every stakeholder benefits equally.

## 4. Evidence Requirements

Relevant evidence may include:

- Published subgroup analyses.
- Studies of differences in AI performance across populations.
- Research on access and digital inclusion.
- Health inequalities research.
- Evaluations of service availability and waiting times.
- Aggregate evidence on geographical or organisational differences.

Where available, the research will record how population groups were defined and whether subgroup findings were sufficiently supported by the study design and sample.

## 5. Ethical and Data Boundaries

The project will use published evidence and appropriately documented aggregate information.

It will not:

- Collect identifiable patient information.
- Attempt to identify individuals from published datasets.
- Infer sensitive characteristics about individuals.
- Upload restricted or confidential datasets to a public repository.
- Make unsupported claims about the performance of AI for particular groups.

The research will follow the access conditions and reuse terms applicable to each source.

## 6. Analytical Principles

The research will:

1. Distinguish average effects from subgroup effects.
2. Consider the distribution of costs as well as benefits.
3. Avoid treating patient populations as homogeneous.
4. Report uncertainty where subgroup evidence is limited.
5. Consider digital access and accessibility.
6. Avoid assuming that unequal outcomes are necessarily caused by AI.
7. Examine whether implementation arrangements may influence the distribution of benefits.

## 7. Limitations

Published studies may not provide sufficiently detailed subgroup information. Differences in population definitions and study methods may also limit comparisons.

The project will identify these limitations and will not present the absence of reported differences as proof of equal outcomes.

## 8. Relationship to the Research Questions

This framework primarily informs RQ5 and RQ6 and contributes to RQ1, RQ2 and RQ4.
