# Revised research scope

## Included

- AI literacy across healthcare professions.
- Clinical interpretation and evaluation of AI outputs.
- Undergraduate, postgraduate and continuing professional education.
- Role-specific AI training.
- Simulation and supervised practice.
- Automation bias, over-reliance and appropriate challenge.
- Human oversight and professional accountability.
- Organisational readiness and implementation.
- Patient communication and trust.
- Clinical accuracy and safety evaluation.
- Training costs and service implications.
- NHS England as the main policy and institutional context.
- International evidence where transferability can be assessed.

## Case study

Diabetic retinopathy screening will illustrate the practical relationship between AI outputs, image quality, diagnostic errors, human review, professional competence and screening pathways.

## Excluded

- Developing or training a new clinical AI algorithm.
- Testing an AI product on real patients.
- Collecting identifiable or confidential patient data.
- Recruiting healthcare workers or patients.
- Claiming that a proposed curriculum is formally accredited.
- Claiming NHS endorsement or institutional approval.
- Treating simulated performance as proof of improved patient outcomes.

## Economic scope

Economic analysis will support decisions about feasibility and resource allocation. The project will not prioritise speculative financial savings over clinical safety and workforce competence.
