# Evidence extraction: Woods et al. (2026)

## Reference

Woods L, Lyons K, Van Der Vegt A, Olsen Q, Huang W, Khor JS, Xu N and Sullivan C (2026).

"Assessing the effectiveness of artificial intelligence education and training for healthcare workers: a systematic review."

*BMC Medical Education*, 26, Article 549.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08969-3

Full text: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12909-026-08969-3

## Research question

What evidence exists on the effectiveness of AI education and training programmes in improving AI literacy among healthcare workers?

## Methods

The authors searched PubMed, Scopus, CINAHL, Embase and ERIC on 20 August 2024.

The review followed PRISMA guidance and was registered with PROSPERO.

## Main findings

Twenty-seven studies were included.

The review found improvements in outcomes corresponding to the first three levels of the Kirkpatrick-Barr training evaluation hierarchy:

- Learner reactions.
- Attitudes and perceptions.
- Knowledge and skills.
- Some reported behavioural changes.

The included programmes did not demonstrate outcomes corresponding to the highest level concerning organisational change and patient benefit.

The review also reports that:

- 44% of programmes were short in duration.
- 56% were delivered in academic settings.
- Doctors and medical students each represented 44% of the reported learner groups.
- 67% focused on introductory AI content.
- Technical AI skills were less frequently addressed.

## Interpretation

The review supports the value of AI education while identifying weaknesses in programme depth and evaluation.

It does not demonstrate that a standardised course improves patient outcomes.

## Relevance to this project

This is a core source for RQ1, RQ2, RQ4 and RQ5.

It supports the argument that training should progress beyond introductory awareness and that its effects should be evaluated at workplace and organisational levels.

## Limitations

The review's search was conducted in August 2024. Newer studies may have been published subsequently. Its included programmes and professional groups were heterogeneous.

## Project decision

Use as a core systematic review of healthcare workforce AI education. Do not interpret improvements in knowledge or attitudes as proof of improved patient outcomes.
