# Quality Appraisal: Diabetic Retinopathy Economic Evidence

## 1. Purpose

This document records an initial appraisal of two sources selected for the diabetic retinopathy case study.

The appraisal distinguishes the quality and relevance of a source from whether its findings can be transferred to NHS England.

## 2. Source DR001: Wang et al. (2024)

**Title:** Economic evaluation for medical artificial intelligence: accuracy vs. cost-effectiveness in a diabetic retinopathy screening case.

**Journal:** npj Digital Medicine.

**DOI:** 10.1038/s41746-024-01032-9.

### Strengths

- Uses a large underlying screening population.
- Examines different AI diagnostic-performance configurations.
- Uses a long-term economic modelling approach.
- Considers both diagnostic accuracy and health economic outcomes.
- Demonstrates that diagnostic accuracy and economic value are not interchangeable.

### Limitations and appraisal concerns

- The economic results depend on model structure and assumptions.
- The study uses a Chinese healthcare setting.
- Costs and service arrangements may differ substantially from NHS England.
- Long-term projections are not equivalent to observed long-term outcomes.
- The detailed parameter values and sensitivity analyses require further examination.

### Initial judgement

The study is highly relevant to understanding the relationship between diagnostic performance and economic value. It is useful for informing model structure and identifying parameters.

Its reported cost estimates should not be directly applied to NHS England without appropriate adaptation.

## 3. Source DR002: Leigh et al. (2025)

**Title:** Health Economic Considerations for the Implementation of Artificial Intelligence-Enabled Diabetic Retinopathy Screening: A Review.

**Journal:** Clinical & Experimental Ophthalmology.

**DOI:** 10.1111/ceo.70016.

### Strengths

- Reviews 18 economic evaluations.
- Examines multiple countries and healthcare settings.
- Discusses cost-effectiveness and cost-utility methods.
- Identifies the importance of context-specific parameters.
- Examines follow-up adherence and diagnostic-performance assumptions.
- Reports the use of established economic reporting guidelines.

### Limitations and appraisal concerns

- The review identifies substantial methodological variation.
- The included studies use different settings, populations and model assumptions.
- The conclusions depend on the quality and completeness of the included evaluations.
- Findings from different economic perspectives should not be treated as directly comparable.

### Initial judgement

This is a highly relevant source for understanding the economic evidence base and identifying methodological weaknesses.

It is particularly useful for informing the appraisal framework and the design of the present research.

## 4. Cross-source assessment

Both sources indicate that economic evaluation of AI-assisted diabetic retinopathy screening requires more than comparing the purchase price of AI with the cost of human grading.

Important considerations include:

- Diagnostic sensitivity and specificity.
- Screening volume and population characteristics.
- Follow-up attendance.
- Referral patterns.
- Technology and implementation costs.
- Staff costs and capacity.
- Long-term health outcomes.
- The economic perspective and time horizon.

## 5. Outstanding appraisal work

The following work remains necessary:

1. Examine the full methods and supplementary material of the Wang study.
2. Identify the sources of its model inputs.
3. Examine the review's included-study table and appraisal methods.
4. Identify UK-specific evidence and cost sources.
5. Assess whether the assumptions required for an NHS-focused model are supported by published evidence.

## 6. Overall conclusion

The two sources provide a useful starting point for a focused economic case study. They do not, by themselves, establish that AI-assisted diabetic retinopathy screening would be cost-effective in NHS England.

The current evidence should therefore be treated as informative but insufficient for a definitive NHS-specific economic conclusion.
