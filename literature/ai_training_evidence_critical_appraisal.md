# Critical appraisal of healthcare AI training evidence

## Purpose

This appraisal considers what published evidence can establish about educating healthcare professionals to use AI.

## Evidence strengths

The Woods et al. (2026) review provides a systematic overview of evaluated AI education programmes for healthcare workers.

The Mofidi and Khoshgoftar (2026) review provides more specific evidence about AI-enhanced virtual patients and clinical reasoning education.

Together, these reviews cover workforce AI literacy and one potentially useful practical training method.

## Evidence limitations

1. Training programmes vary in content, duration and delivery.
2. Professional groups and learner experience differ.
3. Many programmes concentrate on introductory AI knowledge.
4. Knowledge improvements do not necessarily demonstrate safe clinical performance.
5. Self-reported confidence may not reflect actual competence.
6. Simulation results do not automatically transfer to workplace behaviour.
7. Evidence of organisational and patient benefit remains limited.
8. AI systems used in educational studies may not represent approved clinical products.

## Implications

The evidence supports structured education and further evaluation. It does not establish a single universally effective curriculum.

A programme should therefore be tailored to the professional role, intended AI application and level of clinical responsibility.

## Research gap

The most important gap for this project is the limited evidence connecting AI training with sustained workplace behaviour, organisational outcomes and patient benefit.

## Overall judgement

There is a reasonable evidence basis for proposing tiered training and simulation. The effectiveness of the proposed combined framework remains to be tested through future evaluation.
