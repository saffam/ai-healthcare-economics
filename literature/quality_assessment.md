
# Evidence Quality Assessment

## Purpose

This document establishes a structured approach to assessing the credibility, methodological quality and relevance of sources included in the research.

## Assessment Dimensions

### 1. Research Design

- Is the study design appropriate for its research question?
- Is the method explained clearly?
- Is the study observational, experimental, economic or qualitative?

### 2. Data Quality

- Is the data source identifiable?
- Is the sample or dataset adequately described?
- Are missing data and measurement limitations discussed?

### 3. Comparator

- Is the comparison appropriate?
- Is AI compared with current practice or another relevant alternative?
- Are differences between comparison groups considered?

### 4. Economic Evaluation

- Are relevant costs included?
- Is the economic perspective stated?
- Are the time horizon and assumptions explained?
- Is uncertainty examined?
- Are opportunity costs considered?

### 5. Clinical and Operational Outcomes

- Are outcomes relevant to healthcare?
- Are improvements measured rather than simply assumed?
- Is the distinction between technical performance and patient benefit maintained?

### 6. Human and Institutional Factors

- Does the research consider professional oversight?
- Are organisational conditions described?
- Are trust and automation-related risks considered where relevant?

### 7. Conflicts of Interest

- Are funding sources disclosed?
- Are author affiliations reported?
- Are commercial interests acknowledged?

### 8. Generalisability

- Is the healthcare setting clearly described?
- Can the findings reasonably apply to NHS England?
- What contextual differences may limit transferability?

## Assessment Outcome

Each source will receive one of the following descriptive assessments:

- Strong methodological relevance.
- Moderate methodological relevance.
- Limited methodological relevance.
- Insufficient information for assessment.

These are project-specific appraisal descriptions, not a validated universal quality score.

## Reporting Principle

Limitations will be documented rather than concealed. A study will not be considered reliable merely because it is peer-reviewed or published by a recognised institution.
