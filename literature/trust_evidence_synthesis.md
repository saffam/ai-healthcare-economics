# Trust in AI-Assisted Healthcare: Evidence Synthesis

## 1. Scope

This document considers trust in AI-assisted clinical decision-making, with emphasis on healthcare professionals and the organisational conditions needed for appropriate reliance.

## 2. Core source

Tun et al. (2025) conducted a systematic review of healthcare workers' trust in AI-based clinical decision-support systems.

The review is relevant to professional trust, but its findings should not be automatically generalised to patients, carers or the public.

## 3. Dimensions of trust

Trust should not be treated as a single attitude. Relevant dimensions include:

- Perceived reliability.
- Clinical validity.
- Transparency and explainability.
- Usability and workflow fit.
- Familiarity and training.
- Accountability.
- Professional autonomy.
- Ability to challenge or override an output.
- Organisational support.
- Monitoring and feedback.

## 4. Appropriate trust

The objective should not be maximum trust or unconditional acceptance. It should be calibrated reliance: the degree of reliance should correspond to the system's demonstrated performance, limitations and intended use.

Over-reliance can lead to automation bias. Under-reliance can prevent a useful system from contributing to care. Both may reduce the value of an implementation.

## 5. Organisational implications

Healthcare organisations require training, clear responsibility, escalation procedures, monitoring and mechanisms for reporting errors. Staff should understand when the system is intended to support judgement and when human review remains necessary.

## 6. Evidence limitations

The reviewed evidence concerns healthcare workers and AI-based clinical decision support broadly. It is not direct evidence that patients trust AI screening, nor does it establish that trust improves clinical outcomes or reduces costs.

## 7. Research implication

Trust should be analysed as a technical, human and organisational condition that can influence implementation and use. It should not be assigned an invented monetary value in the economic model.
