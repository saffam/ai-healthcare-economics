# AI Implementation in Healthcare: Evidence Synthesis

## 1. Scope

This document considers the organisational and institutional conditions that influence whether AI technologies can be implemented and maintained in healthcare.

## 2. Core source

Powis et al. (2026) describe a framework for AI implementation research in healthcare. The framework consolidates barriers and facilitators across design and development, organisation and culture, deployment and maintenance, quality and safety, and equity.

## 3. Implementation dimensions

### Design and development

Relevant considerations include data quality, model performance, intended use, regulation and suitability for the target population.

### Organisation and culture

Relevant considerations include leadership support, resources, organisational readiness, staff engagement and compatibility with existing professional practice.

### Deployment and maintenance

Relevant considerations include workflow integration, interoperability, user-centred design, monitoring, maintenance and updates.

### Quality and safety

A system requires ongoing assessment of performance, safety, errors, incidents and changes in its operating environment.

### Equity

Evaluation should consider whether performance and access differ across relevant population groups and whether implementation creates or worsens disparities.

## 4. Economic implications

Implementation conditions can affect both costs and benefits. Poor integration may increase staff workload; insufficient training may reduce effective use; weak monitoring may allow performance problems to persist; and unequal access may distribute benefits unevenly.

These effects should be considered in economic evaluation where measurable evidence exists.

## 5. Limitations

An implementation framework is not proof that a particular AI system improves outcomes or is cost-effective. It identifies domains to assess and does not replace intervention-specific evaluation.

## 6. Research implication

Institutional context should be treated as a potential explanation for variation in AI outcomes rather than as background information.
