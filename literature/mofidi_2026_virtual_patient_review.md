# Evidence extraction: Mofidi and Khoshgoftar (2026)

## Reference

Mofidi SA and Khoshgoftar Z (2026).

"Effectiveness of AI-enhanced virtual patients in clinical reasoning training of healthcare professionals and students: a systematic review."

*BMC Medical Education*, 26, Article 1901.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-10242-6

Full text: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12909-026-10242-6

## Research purpose

The review examines whether AI-enhanced virtual patients can support clinical reasoning education among healthcare professionals and students.

## Methods

The authors searched PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus and ERIC.

They included 16 studies involving 1,136 participants. Two reviewers conducted screening and the authors used the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool 2018 for methodological quality assessment.

## Findings

The authors report:

- Ten studies (63%) favoured AI.
- Two studies (13%) reported mixed findings.
- Four studies (25%) were classified as not applicable to the comparison.

The strongest reported direction of findings concerned clinical reasoning and history-taking: seven of eight studies favoured AI.

For other outcomes:

- Learner perception or satisfaction: three of five studies favoured AI.
- Clinical competence or skills: three of five favoured AI.
- Diagnostic accuracy: one of three favoured AI.
- Knowledge or retention: three of three favoured AI.

The authors also found promising results for AI systems used as tutors, mentors and feedback generators.

## Interpretation

AI-enhanced virtual patients may offer repeatable opportunities to practise clinical reasoning, history-taking and feedback.

However, the evidence is not sufficient to conclude that virtual-patient training reliably improves diagnostic accuracy or patient outcomes.

## Relevance to this project

This review supports investigating simulation and virtual-patient learning as components of a broader professional education programme.

## Project decision

Use as supporting evidence for simulation-based education. Do not describe AI virtual patients as validated replacements for clinical supervision or real clinical experience.
