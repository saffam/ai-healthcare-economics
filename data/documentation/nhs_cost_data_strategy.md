# NHS Cost Data Strategy

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the approach to obtaining cost inputs for the diabetic retinopathy screening economic analysis.

## 2. Main source

The NHS England National Cost Collection provides aggregated costs for defined NHS services and patient-level cost information.

The 2023/24 publication is registered as a potential source.

## 3. Important distinction

The National Cost Collection does not automatically provide a directly usable unit cost for every screening activity or AI implementation component.

A general ophthalmology outpatient cost must not be presented as the cost of retinal image grading unless the service definition supports that interpretation.

## 4. Required cost categories

The research will seek evidence for:

- Screening appointment costs.
- Retinal image acquisition.
- Human image grading.
- Clinical review.
- Referral and consultation.
- Repeat imaging.
- Digital surveillance.
- Software licensing.
- Hardware.
- Integration.
- Training.
- Maintenance.
- Cybersecurity.
- Quality assurance.

## 5. Cost year

Every cost value must have a price year.

Where a cost is adjusted to another year, the adjustment method and inflation index must be recorded.

## 6. Missing costs

If a suitable cost cannot be identified, the parameter will remain missing.

A generic or unrelated NHS cost will not be substituted without a justified mapping.

## 7. International cost evidence

International costs may inform the structure of the model but should not be directly described as NHS costs.

Any currency conversion, purchasing-power adjustment or inflation adjustment must be documented separately.

## 8. Current status

The existence of an official NHS costing dataset has been verified. The extraction of specific screening-related unit costs remains outstanding.

No NHS AI screening cost estimate is currently established by this document.
