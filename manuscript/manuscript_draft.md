## Case Study: AI-Assisted Diabetic Retinopathy Screening

To provide a focused application of the research framework, the study examines AI-assisted diabetic retinopathy screening. This application allows the economic implications of AI-supported diagnostic pathways to be considered through a defined clinical activity involving image acquisition, diagnostic classification, referral and follow-up.

The case study compares AI-assisted screening with conventional human grading. The analysis considers technology costs, implementation requirements, workforce implications, diagnostic performance, referral patterns and potential longer-term health outcomes.

The international economic evidence demonstrates that the relationship between diagnostic performance and economic value is not straightforward. Wang et al. (2024), using a model of a nationwide Chinese screening programme, found that alternative AI performance configurations could produce different economic outcomes. Their findings illustrate that the most accurate configuration is not necessarily the most economically efficient.

A review by Leigh et al. (2025) identified 18 economic evaluations of AI-assisted diabetic retinopathy screening. The review highlighted variation in methods, the importance of context-specific model parameters and the potential importance of patient adherence to ophthalmology follow-up.

These findings support the use of a transparent economic framework that considers both clinical performance and the resources required to deliver the screening pathway. They also indicate that economic conclusions may depend on local screening arrangements, labour costs, population characteristics and follow-up behaviour.

NHS England is the principal policy reference point for the present research. However, international study findings will not be treated as direct estimates of NHS costs or outcomes. NHS-specific parameters will be identified separately wherever possible. Where appropriate UK evidence is unavailable, the limitation will be reported rather than concealed through unsupported assumptions.

The case study is intended to demonstrate how the wider research framework can be applied to a particular healthcare activity. It does not establish that AI-assisted screening is universally cost-effective or that findings can be generalised to other healthcare applications.
